# 🚀 Glyde — Go-To-Market & Customer Acquisition

---

## The Core Insight

Glyde's B2B and B2C sides are not separate marketing problems. They are one problem.

**Venues are the distribution channel for attendees.**

Every venue that partners with Glyde puts the QR code on wristbands, tickets, and signage — and automatically drives thousands of B2C users to the app. You don't need to advertise to attendees directly. You acquire venues, and venues bring their crowds.

This means **all early marketing energy should go into B2B acquisition.** B2C follows automatically.

---

## Phase 1 — Validation (Months 1–3)

**Goal:** One real event. Not revenue. Proof.

### What to do
- Build the demo to a point where you can put it in someone's hands and they immediately understand it
- The demo is already started at https://05executor05.github.io/Glyde
- Identify 3 target venues for a free pilot
- Run one real pilot event — even if small (a university event, a local food market, a club night)

### What a pilot gives you
- Real user feedback you can't get from a demo
- Photos and video of the app being used at a real event
- A case study to show every future venue
- Proof that the core concept works

### Where to find your first venue

**Option A — Your own network**
The fastest path. Anyone you know who organises events, works in hospitality, manages a venue, or runs a festival. One warm introduction is worth 100 cold emails.

**Option B — Small local events**
University events, local food festivals, charity runs, outdoor markets, sports club events. These organisers are often one person who wears every hat — they will love Glyde because it genuinely makes their life easier.

**Option C — Show up**
Attend a small local festival as a visitor. Find the organiser on the day. Show them the app on your phone. Offer to run it at their next event for free. This is the most direct approach and it works.

**Option D — LinkedIn outreach**
Search "festival organiser", "event manager", "venue operations" on LinkedIn. Send a short direct message with your demo link. Do not send a pitch deck — send a link they can tap right now.

## Phase 2 — First Paying Customers (Months 4–6)

**Goal:** £1,000 in revenue. Two paying venues.

### The sales pitch
You now have a pilot case study. The pitch is simple:

> "We ran Glyde at [Event Name]. [X] attendees used it. Staff resolved [Y] reports. The food stalls saw a [Z]% increase in orders. Here's what it looked like. Can I show you how it would work at your event?"

That is the entire pitch. Evidence beats features every time.

### Who to target
- Boutique music festivals (1,000–5,000 attendance)
- University events and student unions
- Food and drink festivals
- Outdoor fitness events (Tough Mudder-style)
- Nightclub events and club nights
- Sports events (local football, rugby, cycling)
- Corporate events and conferences **[Claude Suggestion]**
- Cultural festivals (art, film, food)

### How to find them

**Event directories:**
- Skiddle — lists thousands of UK events with organiser contacts
- Eventbrite — contact event organisers directly through the platform
- Resident Advisor (RA) — for music and nightlife events
- Festicket — festival listings with organiser details
- Facebook Events — local events with organiser pages

**Trade bodies and associations:**
- Association of Independent Festivals (AIF) — member directory
- UK Music — industry contacts
- National Outdoor Events Association (NOEA)
- The Events Industry Forum

### Pricing for early customers
Keep it simple. Flat fee, pay after the event.

- "Pay us £[X] after the event. If you don't think it was worth it, don't pay."
- This removes all risk from the venue's decision
- You will get paid every time because the product works

## Phase 3 — Scale (Month 7+)

**Goal:** 20+ events per year. Annual contracts. First white-label deal.

### Content marketing [Claude Suggestion]
- Write short case studies after every event (with venue permission)
- Post on LinkedIn targeting event industry professionals
- Topics: "How we helped X festival resolve 47 toilet complaints before they hit Twitter" — specific, results-focused
- Share photos and video from events (great visual content)

### PR [Claude Suggestion]
- Pitch to event industry trade press: Festival Insights, Access All Areas, Event Magazine
- Story angle: "The app that replaces the festival site map" or "How technology is improving event safety"
- A single feature in Event Magazine reaches thousands of festival organisers

### Industry events [Claude Suggestion]
- Attend: Event Tech Live, International Festival Forum (IFF), The Showman's Show
- These are the events where festival organisers go to discover new tools
- A demo stand or even just attending as a visitor and networking is valuable

### Referral programme [Claude Suggestion]
- Happy venues refer other venues
- Offer: introduce us to another organiser who signs up → get one free event credit
- This is the highest-quality lead source because it comes with a recommendation

### Partnerships [Claude Suggestion]
- **Ticketing platforms:** Skiddle, Eventbrite, DICE — venues already trust them; a partnership brings Glyde into existing onboarding flows
- **Event production companies:** Companies that build and manage festivals often work with multiple organisers — one relationship = many venues
- **Wristband providers:** RFID wristband companies are already integrated into events; bundling Glyde with the wristband makes adoption instant
- **Payment terminal providers:** SumUp, iZettle at events — potential integration play

## B2C Acquisition (Attendees)

As stated, B2C follows from B2B. But there are things to do to increase attendee adoption once inside an event.

### At the event (venue does this, Glyde provides materials)
- QR code on every wristband
- QR code at venue entrance on a large sign
- QR code on the physical programme/map
- Venue announces the app from the stage **[Claude Suggestion]**
- Staff wearing Glyde t-shirts or badges at entrance **[Claude Suggestion]**
- "Win XP by joining Glyde" incentive at the entrance **[Claude Suggestion]**

### Social media (Glyde's own channels) [Claude Suggestion]
- TikTok and Instagram content showing the app in action at real events
- The Memories recap feature is naturally shareable — every attendee who shares their recap is free advertising
- User-generated content from attendees tagging Glyde in their event posts

### Influencer strategy [Claude Suggestion]
- Partner with festival-going creators on TikTok and Instagram
- Not mega-influencers — micro-influencers (10k–100k followers) who genuinely attend festivals
- Give them early access and let them document the experience naturally
- Cost: free tickets + Glyde premium access, no paid fee initially

### App Store optimisation [Claude Suggestion]
- Once on the App Store, optimise for searches like "festival app", "event navigation", "festival map"
- Encourage attendees to leave reviews after events
- Star rating is a key factor in App Store discovery

## Competitive Positioning

### Who else is in this space?

| Competitor | What they do | Glyde's advantage |
|---|---|---|
| Google Maps | General outdoor navigation | No indoor/venue maps, no event features |
| Eventbrite | Ticketing and event discovery | Ticketing only — no in-event experience |
| Festicket | Festival packages and discovery | Pre-event only, no in-event tools |
| Bespoke festival apps | Custom app per festival | Expensive, no cross-event features, no XP, no network effects |
| Whova | Conference/corporate event app | B2B-only, no consumer experience layer, no ordering, no gamification |

### Glyde's positioning statement
> "Glyde is the first app that makes going to events as good as the event itself."

Not a navigation app. Not a ticketing app. The companion that lives with you from the moment you arrive to the moment you share your recap.

## Key Metrics to Track

### B2B metrics
- Number of events on platform (monthly)
- Revenue per event (average)
- Venue churn rate (did they come back for their next event?)
- Sales cycle length (days from first contact to signed deal)
- Net Promoter Score from venue managers

### B2C metrics
- App adoption rate at events (% of attendees who join Glyde)
- Session length (how long are users active in the app per event)
- Feature adoption (% of users who use food ordering, XP, reporting, etc.)
- Retention (do users come back to Glyde at their next event?)
- Organic growth (how many attendees find Glyde through a venue vs directly)

### Product metrics
- Report resolution time (average time from report to resolved)
- Order completion rate (orders placed vs collected)
- Map engagement (% of users who navigate to a POI)
- XP completion rate (% who earn at least one badge)

## Sales Process (B2B)

```
Step 1: Identify target venue or event organiser
Step 2: Send personalised outreach with demo link
Step 3: 20-minute video call — show the app live, ask about their pain points
Step 4: Send a one-page proposal with specific pricing for their event
Step 5: Follow up once (don't spam)
Step 6: If they say yes — onboarding call to set up their event on Glyde
Step 7: Run the event — be available on the day for support
Step 8: Post-event debrief — share analytics, ask for feedback, ask for referral
```

**The sales cycle for small events is fast — days, not months.**  
The sales cycle for major festivals is slow — months, requires multiple stakeholders.  
Start with small events. Build proof. Then approach the big ones.
